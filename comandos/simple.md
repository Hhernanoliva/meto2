# /simple — say that again, for me

The last answer was written for the wrong reader. Say it again for the right one.

## Who the right reader is

Find out, in this order, and stop at the first one that answers:

1. `AGENTS.md` › **Collaboration Style** and **Human Language** in this project.
2. The user's own global instructions file, if it says who they are.
3. If neither says anything: assume the person is **not** an engineer, and say so
   in one line at the end so they can correct it.

## How to say it again

- **Same content, no retreat.** Do not drop the hard parts, do not hedge them into
  vagueness, do not shorten the answer to make it look simpler. If it was true
  before, it is still true.
- **Every term either goes or gets translated the first time it appears.** Not in
  a glossary at the bottom — right there, in the sentence.
- **Lead with what it means for them**: what changes, what they will see, what
  they have to decide. The internals come after, if at all.
- **Analogy before mechanism.** One good comparison beats three correct sentences
  nobody finishes.
- **Numbers only when they change a decision.** "The tests pass" is a status.
  "Nothing you can click is broken" is an answer.
- If part of it genuinely cannot be simplified, **say which part and why** — that
  is information, not an excuse.

## Then

Ask, in one short line, whether that landed or which part is still fog. Take the
answer as a correction to how you write for the rest of this session, not as a
one-time favour.

**Do not apologise, do not preface, do not explain that you are re-explaining.**
Just give the good version.
